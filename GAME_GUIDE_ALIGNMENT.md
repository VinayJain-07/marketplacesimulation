# Marketplace Game Guide Alignment

## Product principle

The simulation should teach students to move through a repeatable decision cycle:

1. Review market, financial, competitor, and stakeholder evidence.
2. Identify the problem or opportunity.
3. Make linked brand, marketing, sales, production, and finance decisions.
4. State the evidence, assumption, and trade-off behind each decision.
5. Run consistency and fatal-error checks.
6. Observe results next quarter and revise the plan.

The interface should retain the Marketplace layout used in the supplied screenshots: quarter navigation on the left, lecture/workspace tabs, a central working area, and quick links, team monitoring, and schedule tools on the right.

## Implemented alignment

- Quarter 4 action plan based on the manual's "Invest in the future" sequence.
- Linked marketing, manufacturing, finance, brand, performance-report, and stakeholder workspaces.
- Advertising claims, media frequency, rebates, and market-research inputs.
- Brand-level demand forecasts, operating-capacity planning, fixed-capacity investment, and maximum-inventory controls.
- Equity, shares, debt, certificate-of-deposit, and live pro forma signals.
- Cross-functional Summary of Decisions.
- Decision-rationale fields that require evidence, assumptions, trade-offs, and monitoring triggers.
- Manual-based Final Check covering price, rebate, capacity, inventory, cash, production feasibility, and rationale completeness.
- Contextual AI Socratic Coach entry points that cost 25 credits per session.

## Recommended learning features

### 1. Decision-outcome ledger

Preserve every material decision, its rationale, the student's forecast, and the actual next-quarter result. Ask the student to explain forecast error before changing the decision. This creates evidence of learning over time instead of evaluating only the final score.

### 2. Competency evidence map

Tag interactions to observable competencies such as financial reasoning, market diagnosis, systems thinking, ethical judgment, forecasting, and communication. Give instructors the source evidence behind every score.

### 3. Adaptive Socratic sampling

Select a small number of high-value decisions for questioning based on risk, inconsistency, abrupt quarter-over-quarter change, and weak rationale. This avoids overwhelming students while still assessing genuine thinking.

### 4. Team decision and dissent log

Allow team members to propose, challenge, and vote on decisions. Preserve minority viewpoints and compare them with actual results. This directly supports teamwork, conflict resolution, and executive communication outcomes.

### 5. Pre-mortem and scenario lab

Before submission, ask teams to model a demand downside, a competitor price response, a capacity disruption, and a stakeholder controversy. Require one contingency trigger and response for each material risk.

### 6. Instructor intervention dashboard

Surface teams with unsupported forecasts, repeated fatal errors, low participation, unexplained strategy shifts, or deteriorating cash. Link every alert to the relevant decision and student explanation.

### 7. Accessibility and multilingual voice assessment

Add captioned voice interactions, keyboard-complete navigation, adjustable speaking speed, transcript review, and multilingual assessment with a shared rubric.

## Recommended implementation order

1. Persist decisions, rationales, and quarter history in a database.
2. Replace demonstration report values with results from the simulation engine.
3. Add the decision-outcome ledger and forecast-error reflection.
4. Add instructor-visible competency evidence and intervention alerts.
5. Add adaptive Socratic sampling and scenario testing.
6. Validate scoring reliability, accessibility, privacy, and academic-integrity controls before high-stakes use.
