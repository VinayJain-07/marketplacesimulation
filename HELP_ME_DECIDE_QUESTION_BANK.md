# Help Me Decide Question Bank

## What this feature is for

`Help me decide` is a guided learning button, not an answer button. It gives students a short Socratic nudge that helps them notice the right evidence, think through trade-offs, and arrive at their own decision.

It is especially useful inside a simulation because students often get stuck on one decision even when the reports already contain the clue. The coach turns that moment into learning instead of guessing.

## Why we added it

We added `Help me decide` so students can:

- connect a decision to a report or result
- learn how to think through uncertainty
- get unstuck without being handed the answer
- practice justification, not memorization
- build confidence before they commit a decision

This is important because different students need different kinds of help. A strong student may need a deeper challenge. An average student may need a clue that points them to the right report. A stuck student may need a smaller, simpler question that reduces the cognitive load.

## Why it costs 25 points

The 25-point cost is there for three reasons:

1. It keeps the coach valuable. Students use it when they really need help, not every time they feel uncertain.
2. It makes the choice meaningful. The student has to decide whether to spend points on guidance or save them for later.
3. It supports a game-like economy. The student learns that insight has value, but the coach is still accessible when needed.

The point cost should feel like a small learning investment, not a penalty. It is enough to encourage care, but not so high that students avoid help completely.

## How the coach should behave

The coach should never give the final answer immediately. It should:

- ask one question at a time
- use the student’s own reports and decisions
- start broad, then get smaller if the student is stuck
- use examples or scenarios when the student asks for the answer directly
- keep responses short and conversational
- adapt the difficulty to the student type

## Student types and coaching style

### Top-performing student

This student already sees the pattern and can explain trade-offs.

What to do:

- ask for evidence
- ask for the hidden risk
- ask what would make them reverse the decision
- ask for transfer to another module

### Average student

This student has part of the answer but not the full explanation.

What to do:

- point them to the relevant report
- ask for one number
- ask what that number means
- ask how the result changes the next decision

### Stuck student

This student does not know where to start.

What to do:

- ask which report they just looked at
- ask whether the issue is demand, cost, cash, or capacity
- narrow to one concrete signal
- then rebuild the explanation step by step

### Student asking for the direct answer

This student wants the coach to choose for them.

What to do:

- do not give the answer
- give a scenario or comparison
- ask what would happen under each option
- let them choose based on the trade-off

## Module-by-module qualitative questions

These are sample prompts the `Help me decide` coach can use in each part of the simulation.

### Market offering / brand setup

- What customer need are you trying to satisfy with this offer?
- Which feature in this product matters most to your target segment?
- If you had to remove one feature, which one would hurt the segment least?
- What would make this offer feel too weak for the segment you chose?
- What is the one signal that tells you this product is positioned correctly?

### Pricing

- Why does this price fit the customer you are targeting?
- What do you think happens to demand if the price moves up by a little?
- What risk are you accepting by setting the price here?
- What report would you check first to see if the price is too aggressive?
- If a competitor lowers price, what would you watch before changing yours?

### Advertising

- What do you want the customer to believe after seeing this ad?
- Which claim in the ad is the strongest one for this segment?
- What would make this message feel unbelievable to the customer?
- How will you know the ad worked beyond just spending the budget?
- If the ad gets attention but not sales, what do you think is missing?

### Sales channel

- Which city or channel is most important for this segment?
- Where do you think the customer is more likely to buy?
- What happens if the sales force is not matched to the segment?
- Which team or region needs more support before demand can grow?
- What would make you shift resources from one city to another?

### Manufacturing

- What do you think happens if demand is stronger than production?
- What risk appears if you produce more than the market buys?
- Which number tells you whether capacity is enough?
- What would make you increase capacity, and what would make you wait?
- How does production affect both profit and cash?

### Finance

- Which financial report gives you the clearest warning sign?
- What would make you worry about cash even if profit looks fine?
- Why does this investment make sense now?
- What debt or liquidity risk are you accepting?
- If you had less cash next quarter, what would you cut first?

### Income statement

- Which cost line changed the most, and why does that matter?
- What part of this statement tells you whether the strategy is working?
- If gross profit falls, what is usually the first thing you should question?
- Which expense feels necessary, and which feels optional?
- What does this statement tell you that sales alone does not?

### Cash flow

- Why can a profitable plan still run into trouble here?
- Which payment comes out before the customer money comes in?
- What part of the quarter is putting the most pressure on cash?
- If cash is tight, what decision would you revisit first?
- What should you watch to avoid a cash surprise?

### Balance sheet

- What does this tell you about the company’s strength right now?
- Which asset is helping the business most this quarter?
- What kind of debt pressure are you willing to accept?
- If liabilities rise, what should you check next?
- What does this report say about flexibility for the next round?

### Balanced scorecard

- Which measure matters most for your current strategy?
- Are you winning on growth, efficiency, or balance?
- What score would worry you most if it fell next quarter?
- Which part of the scorecard shows hidden weakness?
- What trade-off is the scorecard warning you about?

### Detailed brand demand report

- Which brand is pulling the most demand and why?
- What does this report say about customer preference by segment?
- Where do you see evidence of stock-outs or missed demand?
- Which brand is strongest, and what makes it strong?
- What change would shift demand away from this brand?

### Strategic graphs

- What trend is getting better, and what trend is getting worse?
- Which graph should you check before changing the plan?
- What does the shape of the line tell you about the quarter?
- If the trend continues, what happens next?
- Which decision might have caused the biggest swing?

### Competitors’ profiles

- What does this competitor do better than you?
- Which competitor should you worry about most in this segment?
- What gap do you notice in price, demand, or brand strength?
- If you were this competitor, what move would you make next?
- What would make your current advantage disappear?

### Brand profitability / brand judgment

- Which brand is earning more, and why?
- Are you choosing growth or margin with this brand?
- What part of the brand design is doing the most work?
- What evidence tells you this brand is worth keeping?
- If profit falls, which brand decision would you revisit first?

### Forecast lab

- What signal are you trusting most in your forecast?
- What would make your forecast too optimistic?
- What would make you shift from expected to conservative?
- Which assumption is the most fragile?
- What would you want to see before you lock the forecast?

### AI Socratic boardroom

- What decision are you most confident about, and why?
- Which decision needs the strongest justification?
- What risk did you accept that the board should notice?
- What would make you change your mind next quarter?
- Which report best supports your explanation?

## Sample questions by learner type

### Top performer

`Why does this decision belong in this segment rather than a lower-cost one?`

`What single result would make you reverse this choice?`

`Which second-order effect are you most concerned about?`

### Average learner

`Which report gave you the clue for this decision?`

`What number from that report matters most?`

`If that number moves, what happens next?`

### Stuck learner

`Which part feels unclear: demand, cost, cash, or capacity?`

`Did you notice anything unusual in the most recent report?`

`What is the simplest signal you can trust right now?`

### Student wants direct answer

`Imagine demand rises, but capacity stays the same. What happens?`

`Now imagine price drops, but demand does not change. What happens?`

`Which option creates the smaller risk for this quarter?`

## Good design rule for every question

Every question should do one of these things:

- point to evidence
- reveal a trade-off
- test an assumption
- compare two options
- connect one decision to another

If a question does not do one of those things, it is probably too vague.

## Short demo examples

### Example 1

Coach: `What report gave you the clue for this price?`

Student: `The demand report.`

Coach: `What number in that report made the choice feel right?`

### Example 2

Coach: `What happens if production is higher than demand?`

Student: `Inventory builds up.`

Coach: `So what would you watch before increasing capacity again?`

### Example 3

Student: `Just tell me what to do.`

Coach: `Imagine two shelves. One is empty because demand was too strong, and one is full because production was too high. Which shelf risk would hurt you more this quarter?`

## Recommendation for implementation

Use the button to start with one high-value question, then branch based on the student’s answer quality:

- strong answer: ask for a revision trigger
- average answer: ask for one number or report
- weak answer: ask a smaller clue question
- direct-answer request: provide a scenario, not the answer

That keeps the coach simple, useful, and aligned with the learning goal.
